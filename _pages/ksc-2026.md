**KSC 2026 Tutorial**

<h2 id="score-based-sde">From Noise to Shapes</h2>

<figure class="ksc-demo">
  <div data-ksc-screen>
    <img src="{{ '/assets/media/ksc-2026/score-glyphs-preview.png' | relative_url }}" width="1080" height="640" alt="Four panels show how noisy pixels resolve into simple shapes.">
  </div>
  <figcaption>
    <a href="{{ '/assets/media/ksc-2026/score-glyphs.mp4' | relative_url }}" data-ksc-video aria-label="Play From Noise to Shapes">Play video</a>
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The pixels start as noise. The outlines of the shapes become visible as a solver steps through the reverse-time SDE. In this example, a Gaussian mixture fitted to simple shapes supplies the score. The score guides the drift, and Brownian increments keep the sampling process stochastic. [Score-based generative modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456)

<h2 id="continuous-normalizing-flow">How a Flow Changes Density</h2>

<figure class="ksc-demo">
  <div data-ksc-screen>
    <img src="{{ '/assets/media/ksc-2026/density-transport-preview.png' | relative_url }}" width="1080" height="640" alt="A point grid contracts on one side as the density concentrates in the matching region.">
  </div>
  <figcaption>
    <a href="{{ '/assets/media/ksc-2026/density-transport.mp4' | relative_url }}" data-ksc-video aria-label="Play How a Flow Changes Density">Play video</a>
  </figcaption>
</figure>

As the flow compresses space, the density rises where points move closer together. A continuous normalizing flow (CNF) integrates an invertible ODE and tracks the log density through the negative divergence of its velocity field. The left panel shows a subset of points from the distribution. The right panel shows the density of the full distribution. [Continuous density estimation with FFJORD](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01367)

<h2 id="flow-matching">Learning a Velocity Field</h2>

<figure class="ksc-demo">
  <div data-ksc-screen>
    <img src="{{ '/assets/media/ksc-2026/helix-flow-matching-preview.png' | relative_url }}" width="1080" height="640" alt="A three-dimensional point cloud separates into two helical strands.">
  </div>
  <figcaption>
    <a href="{{ '/assets/media/ksc-2026/helix-flow-matching.mp4' | relative_url }}" data-ksc-video aria-label="Play Learning a Velocity Field">Play video</a>
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The point cloud winds into two strands as the solver integrates a learned velocity field. Flow matching trains that field against velocities along paths between noise and target samples. Here the strand label is supplied as a condition during both training and sampling. [Flow Matching for Generative Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747)

<h2 id="comparison">What the Solver Integrates</h2>

<table class="ksc-methods">
  <thead>
    <tr><th scope="col">Method</th><th scope="col">State update</th><th scope="col">Density</th><th scope="col">Noise</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><th scope="row">Score-based SDE</th><td>Reverse-time drift and diffusion</td><td>The score gives the gradient of log density</td><td>Initial noise and Brownian increments</td></tr>
    <tr><th scope="row">CNF</th><td>Invertible ODE</td><td>Integrates the negative divergence</td><td>Random starting samples in this example</td></tr>
    <tr><th scope="row">Flow matching</th><td>ODE with a learned velocity field</td><td>Density can be tracked through the resulting CNF</td><td>Random starting samples in this example</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

A state-space model separates the evolving hidden state from the observations it produces. A continuous-time state update describes what happens between observation times. In the generative examples here, the terminal state gives the sample.
