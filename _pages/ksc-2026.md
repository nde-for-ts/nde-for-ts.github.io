**KSC 2026 Tutorial** · YongKyung Oh (Pusan National University)

## Abstract

Neural differential equations use neural networks to model dynamics in continuous time. This tutorial covers Neural ODEs, CDEs, and SDEs, with applications to irregular clinical and sensor observations, physical and biological systems, and generative modeling. Short code examples illustrate how to train and solve these models. The tutorial closes with practical guidance on choosing a model for a given application.

## Foundations of Continuous-Time Models

A Neural ODE learns how the state changes and uses a numerical solver to compute its trajectory. Solver choice, stiffness, and adjoint methods affect the cost of learning these dynamics. The two examples below show how to constrain a velocity field and how to differentiate through a simulation.

## Irregular Observations and Uncertainty

A Neural CDE interpolates the observed values into an input path that drives the hidden state. Including time as a channel preserves the actual spacing between observations. A Neural SDE adds a diffusion term, so repeated forecasts from the same last observation can follow different trajectories.

## From Continuous Dynamics to Generative Models

A continuous normalizing flow (CNF) uses an invertible ODE to transform samples and track their probability density. Flow matching is a way to train the ODE's velocity field by regressing against velocities along paths between source and target samples. A field trained this way can also be used as a CNF.

## Choosing a Model

Start with the structure of the problem. An ODE can describe deterministic dynamics or transform a representation. A CDE lets the observation path drive the state. An SDE can model a distribution of possible trajectories. For generation, choose the training objective and sampler together, and check the cost and accuracy of the numerical solver.
