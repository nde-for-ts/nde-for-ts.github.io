Neural Differential Equations for Generative Modeling

How do continuous-time models turn noise into new samples?

KSC 2026 Tutorial

From Noise to Shapes

The pixels start as noise. The outlines of the shapes become visible as a solver steps through the reverse-time SDE. In this example, a Gaussian mixture fitted to simple shapes supplies the score. The score guides the drift, and Brownian increments keep the sampling process stochastic. Score-based generative modeling

How a Flow Changes Density

As the flow compresses space, the density rises where points move closer together. A continuous normalizing flow (CNF) integrates an invertible ODE and tracks the log density through the negative divergence of its velocity field. The left panel shows a subset of points from the distribution. The right panel shows the density of the full distribution. Continuous density estimation with FFJORD

Learning a Velocity Field

The point cloud winds into two strands as the solver integrates a learned velocity field. Flow matching trains that field against velocities along paths between noise and target samples. Here the strand label is supplied as a condition during both training and sampling. Flow Matching for Generative Modeling

A state-space model separates the evolving hidden state from the observations it produces. A continuous-time state update describes what happens between observation times. In the generative examples here, the terminal state gives the sample.
